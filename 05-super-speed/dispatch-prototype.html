    Presenter notes

    What happened after 4.2. The release shortened the response window from 90 to 60 seconds and increased the weight of travel time in ranking. An expired ping still deducted 0.12 from acceptance history, exactly like an explicit decline, with no automatic recovery. This creates a feedback loop where missing an offer can lower someone’s rank and reduce future offers. In equal 21-day windows excluding release day, acceptance fell from 77.5% to 62.4%, misses rose from 12 to 91, and unaccepted callouts rose from 23/430 to 44/345. Farlight’s weekly pings fell from 11–13 to 3, 1 and 0. The data and code establish the pattern and mechanism, but missing delivery timestamps and candidate ranks prevent us from isolating the contribution of each release change.

    1. Offer expires after 60 seconds
    2. →Acceptance score drops by 0.12
    3. →Dispatch tries the next responder
    4. →Lower score can reduce future offers

    What the proposal addresses. Preserve acceptance history when an offer expires, while continuing to the next responder. This prevents that miss from lowering the responder’s rank on later callouts. Kip sees separate expired, offered, accepted and unassigned events, and Farlight sees when an offer has ended. The roster explains the ranking inputs to Helen. In the illustrative second callout, preserving Farlight’s 0.84 acceptance score produces a total of 0.840, ahead of Cindermark’s 0.820. Applying the current miss penalty produces 0.810 and puts Farlight behind. Future offers still depend on availability, travel time and skills. Recovery for already-low scores and notification delivery need separate decisions.

    1. Offer expires after 60 seconds
    2. →Acceptance score stays the same
    3. →Dispatch tries the next responder
    4. →Future ranking uses the preserved score

    How we will judge success. First, validate that handlers can correctly explain where a callout went and responders understand the expired-offer state. Before a release trial, agree with Helen, Wen and Ravi on targets and a comparison design, using a control group where feasible. Capture candidate ranks, availability, delivery timing and each ping’s outcome. Verify that misses preserve scores and explicit declines deduct 0.12. Compare weekly first-offer share and pings per available hour for responders who miss offers, alongside acceptance rate, misses and declines separately, unaccepted-callout rate and median time to assignment. Success means fewer responders lose future opportunities after misses, fewer callouts remain unaccepted, and assignment speed stays within the agreed limit. Review responder-level results as well as the overall average. A rise in acceptance alone would leave the quiet-responder problem unresolved.
